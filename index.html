01

CT registration with PnP & continuous scaling

Monocular SLAM reconstructs the scene and camera path at an unknown scale, in a coordinate system separate from the patient's CT. Navigation requires both the camera pose and reconstructed surface to be aligned with the CT anatomy. Direct surface registration is difficult because the reconstruction is partial and its scale is unknown.

My contribution

I built a CT rendering module to set an initial camera estimate, then displayed the endoscopic frame beside a CT rendering using the same camera calibration. I developed the correspondence-selection module to associate image points with 3D CT locations, and used RANSAC-based PnP to estimate pose while rejecting inconsistent correspondences.

Scale estimation & visualization

I built a continuous scaling pipeline that compares reconstructed 3D points associated with tracked image points against locations obtained by ray tracing into the CT mesh. This brings the reconstruction into CT scale. I integrated the registered camera pose into 3D Slicer using existing visualization modules.

Next challenge

Manual correspondence selection must be repeated after the endoscope is removed and navigation restarts. This motivated the automatic re-registration project below.

02

Automatic CT re-registration after endoscope reinsertion

Surgeons may remove an endoscope to clean the lens or switch tools. When the camera returns, navigation must recover its alignment with CT. Repeating manual correspondence selection interrupts the workflow; this project aims to recover that alignment automatically.

Method

The system matches the current camera image to a previously observed frame with a known pose. That prior pose supplies a CT rendering for image matching and geometric pose estimation, replacing the manually chosen initial view used in the first project.

My contribution

I adapted my existing registration pipeline to use the current frame together with a matched prior frame and a CT rendering generated from the prior pose. This connects prior-image retrieval to the registration workflow so navigation can be reinitialized without selecting correspondences manually each time.

03

Tissue mesh mosaicking for robotic scanning

In this ARCADE Lab project, a robotic endoscope is being developed to visually survey tissue before closer ultrasound and fluorescence imaging. The ultrasound scan needs a consistent distance from the tissue, whose surface contains peaks and valleys. A 3D surface model is intended to support that motion planning.

Technical challenge

A stereo video sequence captures local views of the surface. Building a mesh of the entire scanned area requires reliable camera poses, metric depth, and a way to join the observations. Robot kinematics alone can introduce positioning errors and drift.

My contribution

I refined a mosaicking pipeline that combines SLAM-based camera trajectories, stereo depth estimation, and surface reconstruction. Camera poses place the depth observations into a common scene; stereo provides metric tissue depth. Marching cubes then extracts a surface mesh from the combined reconstruction.

Smith+Nephew-sponsored design project · Design Day 2026

CapSeal: hip capsule closure

Hip arthroscopy requires surgeons to manipulate and reapproximate the capsule surrounding the joint. The CapSeal project addresses the difficulty of controlling capsular flaps and passing sutures during closure.

Design challenge

Surgeon-driven instruments require manual force and precise wrist motion. Device-driven systems can reduce those demands, but may constrain approach angles or require extra loading and retrieval steps. Our goal was to simplify closure while supporting precise tissue handling.

My contribution

As design team lead, I coordinated clinicians and industry mentors, analyzed surgical workflows, and developed tool concepts through iterative prototyping. I created CAD models and technical drawings in PTC Creo.

Featured publication · Second author · 2026

A benchtop model for preliminary assessment of penile clamp effects on biofluid mechanical changes in male urinary incontinence

Goyal, T., Chan, C.H.R., et al. Medical Engineering & Physics 147, 085007 (2026).

The study compares four commercial clamps using load-cell force measurements and a foam model with separate urinary and venous flow channels. Two foam stiffnesses provide distinct mechanical conditions for testing how compression affects both flows.

Clamp geometry and pressure distribution influenced performance beyond average contact stress alone. The model provides a controlled framework for preliminary device comparisons; its simplified anatomy and flow conditions require further validation before clinical conclusions.
